AlignED as a product: users, trade-offs and what I would measure

AlignED started as an analysis and became a small product. This is the product thinking behind it, adapted from the brief on the app's About page.

The problem

Students choose graduate programs, and decide which skills to learn next, without evidence of how the curriculum compares with hiring demand. “Industry-ready” is usually claimed, not measured.

Who it is for, and the job to be done

Users: graduate students choosing or finishing a program, and academic advisors or program offices reviewing a curriculum.

Job to be done: “Show me, with evidence, which skills employers ask for that my program does not cover, and what to learn first.”

Three entry points, by user need

  • See a program's gaps.
  • Check one job posting against your skills.
  • Analyze your own course list. The same tested analysis runs on any course list supplied as CSV or pasted text, with nothing stored.

Decisions I made on evidence

  • A simpler, explainable extractor over an LLM that scored lower. The LLM won on the data it was tuned on and lost on 50 unseen items. The full story is in this note.
  • A fixed sample over a live feed, so published numbers do not drift. Fresh data appears separately as a weekly market pulse.
  • Fewer, tested gaps over a longer list. Multiple-comparison correction cut significant gaps from 236 to 170, and demand trends from 9 to 0. The app shows the stronger claim, not the bigger one.

A data limit that nearly changed a decision

Newer job postings come with only the first 500 characters of each description. Compared naively with the full-text sample, Python appeared to fall from 40% to 7% of postings, which would have looked like a major market shift. Measured on the same 500 characters, the difference vanished. I re-measured the historical sample on the same basis before comparing.

What I would measure next

These are proposals. The app has no analytics, so I claim no usage numbers.

  • Share of visitors who complete a gap analysis or a job match.
  • Return visits after the weekly refresh.
  • Advisors who say they would use it in a curriculum review.

Known limits

Keyword matching misses broad skill areas. Course descriptions are a proxy for what is actually taught. The posting sample is not the whole market, and 50 held-out items is small.

Next

A larger skill vocabulary, a second independent labeler, and a short user study with advisors.
